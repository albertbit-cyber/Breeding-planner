## 4. Which country's law applies

If there's ever a dispute, this decides whose law is used and which courts hear it.

**Recommended: the country where you (or your company) are based.** Choosing somewhere
else is usually a bad idea — it makes disputes more expensive for you, and against
consumers it often doesn't hold up anyway, because EU and UK consumers keep the right to
sue in their own country regardless of what your terms say.

## What happens next

1. I fill every blank in both documents and remove the yellow highlighting.
2. I re-check that nothing else in them has drifted from what the code does.
3. You publish, or send them to a lawyer first.

**On the lawyer:** I'd still recommend one before you take payment. Not because the
documents are weak — they now describe your software accurately, which is the part most
templates get wrong — but because a few things genuinely need a qualified opinion in your
specific country:

- Whether your liability caps hold up where you're based.
- Consumer withdrawal rights (EU/UK buyers can cancel a digital service within 14 days,
  with specific rules about waiving that to get instant access — the subscription section
  doesn't handle this in detail yet).
- Whether the genetics-prediction disclaimer is enforceable in your jurisdiction. This is
  the clause most likely to matter to you commercially, so it's worth the money.

A lawyer reviewing an accurate draft is a much smaller bill than one writing from scratch.
