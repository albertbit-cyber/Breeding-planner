The read

What the app is today

Serpentora is not a morph calculator with records bolted on. It is a full breeding operations system with four connected surfaces, and the genetics engine is only the front door.

Breeder app

The flagship. Animals, genetics, pairings, clutches, incubation, pedigree, quarantine, racks, calendar, exports, backups.

  • Runs in a browser, as a Windows desktop app, and as Android and iOS builds from the same code
  • Local-first: it keeps working with no connection

Lab portal

Partner labs receive orders, log samples, enter results and issue verifiable certificates that land back in the breeder's records.

  • Order a test from inside the animal's own record
  • Certificates carry a number and a verification code

Marketplace

Stores, listings, inquiries, favourites, saved searches, reviews and sales — attached to animals you already track.

  • Spinning out as its own product; mention it as a direction, not a feature

Admin back office

Users, verification, subscription tiers, reports, lab accounts, announcements, GDPR tooling.

  • Invisible to buyers, but it is why the trust claims on the site are honest
The audit

Functionality inventory

Twelve capability groups found in the code. The chip on each says how far you can push it publicly: In the app means a breeder can use it now, Built, unsurfaced means it exists but needs a screen or the site to make it visible, Hold means don't put it on the site yet.

Animal records

In the app

The database underneath everything else.

  • Paste a messy animal description and the quick-add parser pulls out ID, sex, genetics, weight, birth date and feeding notes
  • Nine log types — feed, weight, shed, cleaning, medication, water, health, notes, other — logged in one tap from the animal card
  • Photos with camera capture, an ID wizard that builds your own ID format, custom status tags, groups, import with a parse preview
  • Exports as PDF, Excel and CSV with your own field selection

Genetics engine

In the app

The wedge feature. This is what people screenshot.

  • 635 curated genes across 21 species — 233 for ball pythons alone — with type, complex, super form, aliases and shorthand
  • Handles recessive, dominant, incomplete dominant, complexes and super forms; flags genes carrying wobble, kinking, infertility or a lethal super
  • Punnett engine gives per-gene odds, combined odds, projected visuals and projected hets
  • Understands how breeders actually write genetics: 66% Het Pied, slashes, commas, combo nicknames and shorthand, with your own alias tables

Breeding projects

In the app

Season-long tracking, not a single calculation.

  • Pairings with generated five-month appointment schedules, recorded locks and per-appointment notes
  • Lifecycle milestones: ovulation, pre-lay shed, clutch date, fertile eggs, slugs, hatch
  • Incubator view builds egg boxes with due dates and remaining time
  • Printable clutch cards and pairing QR labels; completed projects archived by year

Breeding Advisor

In the app

The most demo-able screen in the product.

  • Type the goal in plain language — "clown desert ghost with pastel and possible het pied"
  • Set required, optional and avoid traits, a minimum probability and weighting
  • Returns your own animals ranked by chance of hitting the goal, with projected visuals, hets and holdback hints
  • Turn a suggestion into a multi-generation breeding plan, or export the shortlist

Family tree

In the app

The professionalism pillar. Sells to anyone who has to prove lineage.

  • Pedigree canvas with animal, clutch and junction nodes, opened straight from any animal
  • Pedigree passport panel for a single animal's ancestry
  • Privacy filters control what is exposed when a tree is shared with a buyer

Reproductive intelligence

Built, unsurfaced

Nothing else on the market does this. It deserves its own site section.

  • Builds a reproductive profile per female from her own logged history
  • Tags her pattern: early or late ovulator, frequent or infrequent locker, consistent or variable timing
  • Predicts the next ovulation and clutch as a date window, labelled with a confidence level so the app never bluffs on thin data

Quarantine module

In the app

Genuinely differentiated and completely absent from competitors.

  • Phase-based quarantine timeline per new arrival, with the next due action surfaced
  • A clearance checklist that will not tick itself: minimum days served, clean recent checks, accepted feeding
  • Reference library of the real diagnostics — what a faecal float or a cloacal swab does and does not rule out, sample type, turnaround, limitations
  • Information only. No prices, no orders, no medical advice — it makes the conversation with your vet better

Racks and spaces

In the app

The "where is this animal physically" answer.

  • Rooms containing heat racks and terrariums, with slot-level assignment
  • Occupancy and empty-slot summaries per room
  • Guards against placing the same animal in two slots at once

Calendar and exports

In the app

Feeds, weights, cleanings, sheds, meds, appointments and clutch actions in one month view.

  • Filter by event type or by sire to isolate one project
  • Near-term breeding reminders built from the appointment schedule
  • Export to Google Calendar (.ics), a full-range calendar file, or an appointment spreadsheet

Lab testing pipeline

In the app

The differentiator no spreadsheet and no competitor has.

  • Order a genetic test from the animal's own record — over 40 morph tests plus sex determination
  • Printable sample labels, live order and payment status, result count on the animal
  • Completed results produce a certificate with a number and a verification code a buyer can check
  • Partner labs work the queue in their own portal: intake, result entry, completed tests, their own catalog and pricing

Mobile and terminal mode

In the app

The rack-side story. Show it as video, not as a feature bullet.

  • QR labels on tubs: scan one and the animal opens on your phone
  • Terminal mode for fast rounds — scan, log a feed or weight, take a photo, move on
  • Full mobile record: overview, feed cycle, details, logs, photos and breeding, plus a mobile rack view
  • Actions queue while offline and sync when you are back on a connection

Your data, your control

In the app

The trust section. Under-sold today, and it costs signups.

  • Local-first: your collection works with no connection and no account
  • Manual backups, scheduled nightly / weekly / monthly backups, and a backup vault with named restore points and retention limits
  • Ten languages including right-to-left Hebrew; theme, palette and font presets
  • Account email verification and recovery, notification preferences, GDPR tooling in the back office
The pitch

What the site must show, in order of persuasive power

Ranked. The order matters — this is the sequence that converts a sceptical breeder, and it is not the order the features were built in. Show the product working before explaining what it is.

1

A live genetics calculator, no signup

Show
A working cross on the page itself: pick a male, pick a female, get real odds. Not a screenshot, not a video — the actual calculation, running on the marketing site.
Why
It is the only feature that proves competence in three seconds, and it is the one thing this community already screenshots into arguments. Every shared result is a link back.
Proof
The Punnett engine and the 635-gene database are self-contained and run entirely in the browser.
Build
Embed the engine with a curated set of morphs. Give every result a shareable link and an image sized for Facebook and Instagram Stories.
2

The Breeding Advisor answering a real question

Show
A twelve-second loop: someone types "I want clown desert ghost with pastel and possible het pied" and their own animals come back ranked by odds.
Why
Every competitor stops at "here is what this pairing makes". Only this answers "which of my animals should I pair". That gap is the product.
Proof
Goal parsing, trait weighting, ranked suggestions with success chance and projected hets, all shipping.
Build
Screen capture against a demo collection. Show the typing — the plain-language input is half the magic.
3

Lab-confirmed genetics landing in the record

Show
The chain in four frames: order a test from the animal, print the sample label, the lab enters the result, a certificate with a verification code appears on the animal.
Why
Nobody else in this category has it. It converts the semi-pro and pro segment, where a disputed het claim is a money and reputation problem.
Proof
Over 40 orderable morph tests, label PDFs, status tracking, certificates with verification codes, a full partner-lab portal.
Build
A four-panel diagram plus one real certificate image with the numbers blurred.
4

Pedigree you can hand to a buyer

Show
A real family tree, three generations deep, with the privacy filter visibly on — proving you can share lineage without exposing your whole collection.
Why
"Proven pair" and "line-bred" claims are currently backed by nothing but a seller's word. This is the professionalism argument.
Proof
Pedigree canvas with clutch nodes, a per-animal passport panel and privacy filters.
Build
One wide screenshot. Let it be big — this feature photographs well and small thumbnails waste it.
5

Predictions for each female, with honest confidence

Show
One female's panel: her pattern tags, her predicted ovulation window, and the confidence badge sitting next to it.
Why
It is the most advanced thing in the product and it is currently invisible. The confidence badge is the selling point, not a caveat — it says the software will tell you when it does not know.
Proof
Reproductive profiles built from logged cycles and lock events, pattern classification, date-window predictions with confidence levels.
Build
Screenshot plus one line: "It learns from her history, and tells you how sure it is."
6

The phone at the rack

Show
Filmed hands: scan the QR label on a tub, the animal opens, log the feed, next tub. Fifteen seconds, no narration.
Why
It answers the real objection to every record-keeping tool — "I will never actually keep it updated."
Proof
QR label export and scanning, terminal mode, one-tap logging, offline queue and sync.
Build
A phone-held video. This is the single highest-value asset to film and it needs no design work.
7

Quarantine done properly

Show
The clearance checklist mid-quarantine, with one item not yet met.
Why
Unclaimed territory, and it speaks to the part of the audience that takes biosecurity seriously — exactly the people other keepers listen to.
Proof
Phase timeline, next-action prompts, a clearance checklist that will not pass early, and a reference library of what each diagnostic actually rules out.
Build
Screenshot plus the honest framing: it is information, not medical advice.
8

Your data stays yours

Show
Three plain lines: works offline, exports to PDF, Excel and CSV whenever you want, backup vault with restore points.
Why
This audience has watched tools die. "You can leave with everything" removes the biggest silent objection to starting.
Proof
Local-first storage, scheduled backups, a named-restore-point vault, and exports across every major record type.
Build
Text only. No illustration needed; a lock icon would weaken it.
9

The breadth, once — as a number, not a grid

Show
One strip: 635 genes, 21 species, 9 log types, 10 languages, and web, desktop, iPhone and Android.
Why
Breadth is the reason to pay, but leading with it reads as a feature dump. Placed late, it turns interest into confidence.
Proof
All of those numbers are counted from the shipping code.
Build
Resist the icon grid. Numbers and words.

Discipline

Don't claim this yet

Everything above is honest today. These are not.

No prices, no trials, no "upgrade"

Payment processing is not live, so the pricing page can describe plans but must not promise a purchase. Until it is, the only action on the site is the waitlist — and the founding-member pricing lock is what makes joining today worth something.

No signup promising stored data until the legal pages exist

The app already links to /terms and /privacy on the public domain and those links are currently broken. They have to exist before any campaign points at a signup form — and shipping them fixes a live bug at the same time.

Don't split the domain before redirecting the account emails

Verification, password reset and email-change links are matched on exact paths. Moving the app to a subdomain without redirecting those three paths from the apex breaks every email already sitting in someone's inbox.

One name, everywhere, before anything public

Parts of the product still say "Breeding Planner". Someone who arrives from a Serpentora ad and sees the old name in the app has a bad first impression in a market that already distrusts new tools. Finish the rename first.

Say "syncs across your devices" carefully

Cloud sync exists and is in daily use, but has open reliability work. Lead with local-first and offline — which is true, differentiating and reassuring — and treat multi-device sync as a supporting line rather than a headline promise.

Marketplace is a direction, not a feature

It is spinning out as its own product. Mentioning it as something coming is fine; putting it in the feature list invites questions the site cannot answer yet.
